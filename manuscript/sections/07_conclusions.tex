\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

\revtext{Selecting the number of clusters on spatial data runs into a property
that the standard criteria share. They take no account of the dependence
between observations, and so cannot separate grouping that reflects discrete
classes from grouping that autocorrelation alone produces. Under spatial
autocorrelation, the gap} statistic and the silhouette, Calinski--Harabasz and
Davies--Bouldin indices reported several classes on every field we simulated
with one, and divided single land-cover regions on real imagery.

\revtext{We have proposed a remedy framed in geostatistical terms: the
problem is recast as a calibrated test of cluster homogeneity, which asks
whether a region carries multimodality in excess of spatial autocorrelation.}
After removing a low-order trend whenever one is resolvable, ESS-Dip evaluates
the Hartigan dip along the locally optimal split direction and calibrates it
against the \emph{effective} sample size. That size is estimated from a
within-class autocorrelation range obtained by local range estimation. \revtext{For a stylised version of the test, we prove that the probability of
dividing a homogeneous region vanishes. The procedure runs in near-linear
time.} On a factorial
simulation with known ground truth it reaches unit specificity, and its recall
variant ESS-Dip-R attains the best balanced score of sixteen criteria at the
same specificity. On the Indian Pines, Salinas and Sentinel-2
scenes, both variants identify single land-cover regions as homogeneous where
the standard criteria do not. \revtext{The asymmetry between its two kinds of error makes the family as
suitable for use as a homogeneity guard or a stopping rule for region-growing
as for selecting the number of clusters. The family spans a precision--recall
axis, from the conservative distribution-free null to the higher-recall
Gaussian one.}

\revtext{The broader message has two parts. First, two long-standing tools of
geostatistics, the effective sample size and declustering, are what a
clustering criterion needs in order to answer this question on dependent data.
Second, the signal that separates classes from autocorrelation is modality
rather than dispersion.} All code and
data required to reproduce the simulation study and the applications are
released.
